# Shape A reader-method contamination and cross-reader scope firewall

If `D` and paired harm `tau` are produced by the same frozen reader/scorer, a positive Shape A result supports at most a fixed-reader structural risk marker. Column isolation, timing controls, negative controls, and cross-fitting do not by themselves exclude common-method variance.

No cross-reader generation is started by default. Only when cross-reader portability was already selected for an independent mechanism reason and a legitimate writer×reader matrix already exists may the same stable IDs, commit boundary, memory, suffix, null/gold outputs, and reader outputs be reused without new generation. Before cross-reader outcomes, freeze the reader set, reference D definition, grouped cross-fitted estimand `g`, direction and SESOI, competence floor, and censor/missingness rules.

The matrix evaluates `D_reader_a → tau_reader_b`. Diagonal and every preregistered off-diagonal cell must use the same complete examples; readers are fixed methods, not iid units. At least two competent readers and at least 24 common examples per cell are required. Reader-dependent missingness is forbidden.

Mechanical outcomes are:

- all diagonal/off-diagonal directions stable with competence, reliability, and shams passing: `FINITE_READER_ROBUST`;
- diagonal only: `COMMON_METHOD_SUSPECT`;
- D unstable across readers: `DEFECT_MEASUREMENT_NONINVARIANT`;
- only one tau-reader column positive: `OUTCOME_METHOD_SPECIFIC`;
- unrelated-target or marginal-pairing shams reproduce the advantage: `COMMON_METHOD_NO_GO`;
- only one competent reader: `FIXED_READER_ONLY`.

Finite-reader robustness never implies latent true state, policy-family sufficiency, cross-architecture generality, human readability, or a deployable gate. This firewall changes claim scope only and cannot unlock training, step 400, C256, external benchmarks, or new generation.
